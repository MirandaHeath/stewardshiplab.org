On 2 Apr, in the
<a href="https://github.com/nvim-treesitter/nvim-treesitter" class="suplink" target="_blank">nvim-treesitter</a>
issue tracker, a user was upset that the changes he wanted to see were not being made.

He angrily took this out on the maintainer, telling him to

<blockquote>
	<cite class="cite--byline">
		<img
			src="https://avatars.githubusercontent.com/u/58133782?v=4"
			alt="sushtain's avatar"
			width="22"
			height="22"
		>
		<a
			href="https://github.com/nvim-treesitter/nvim-treesitter/discussions/8627#discussioncomment-16440673"
			target="_blank"
		>shushtain on 2 Apr</a>
	</cite>
	<p>
		go switch to something that doesn't require interacting with people
	</p>
</blockquote>

The maintainer, having already been overwhelmed with issues and complaints from entitled users, replied

<blockquote>
	<cite class="cite--byline">
		<img
			src="https://avatars.githubusercontent.com/u/2361214?v=4"
			alt="clason's avatar"
			width="22"
			height="22"
		>
		<a
			href="https://github.com/nvim-treesitter/nvim-treesitter/discussions/8627#discussioncomment-16442595"
			target="_blank"
		>clason on 2 Apr</a>
	</cite>
	<p>
		OK
	</p>
</blockquote>

Now, the banner at the top of nvim-treesitter's
<a href="https://github.com/nvim-treesitter/nvim-treesitter" target="_blank" class="suplink">GitHub page</a>
reads:

<blockquote>
	<em>This repository was archived by the owner on Apr 3, 2026. It is now read-only.</em>
</blockquote>

That's the end of nvim-treesitter. At least, for now.

But more importantly, we've driven away another person whose contributions we should cherish.

This is a regular occurence, as Miranda Heath's [report on burnout in Open Source][burnout-report] has shown us. We
don't know the full story of Christian Clason, the maintainer of nvim-treesitter. But we have stories from people like
Ashley Williams:

<blockquote>
	<p>
		I was doing nights and weekends, it was wrecking my health and I was just devastated, so after a while, enough
		issue follow-ups, like, “is this maintained?”, “are you gonna fix my issue?”, I had to say that I had to give it
		up.
	</p>
	<cite>
		Ashley Williams, p 8 of the
		<a href="https://mirandaheath.website/static/oss_burnout_report_mh_25.pdf" target="_blank">
			burnout report
		</a>, and
		<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gR2hjbh0RUk" target="_blank">
			on YouTube
		</a>
	</cite>
</blockquote>

Explaining these situations by referring to the faults of any individual person would be a mistake, because the problem
is systemic.

Users of Open Source don't have enough educational resources to understand how to healthily and sustainably engage with
the maintainers they rely on.

And maintainers do not have the know-how, or the direct support, to care for their mental health when they're in the
middle of a deluge of issues and PRs.

## What Do We Do?

Miranda is working on a follow-up study of mental health among Open Source maintainers, as part of the
[Software Stewardship Lab][lab], a new non-profit working to make the global Open Source ecosystem more sustainable.

Are you an Open Source maintainer that wants to share their story? [Contact Miranda](https://mirandaheath.website).

[Bluesky](https://bsky.app/profile/mirandaheath.website) or

[Mastodon](https://fosstodon.org/@stewardshiplab),
and you can come talk to us
[on Discord](https://discord.gg/2GNQHcKmcH).
